\begin{abstract}
Intra-weight injection lets one model change another model’s behaviour by editing its weights without gradients. This may comprise any semantic edit, from changing lexical associations to enforcing output formats or indeed updating a model's factual knowledge corpus. Yet despite interest in these approaches, no systematic method has existed to measure and evaluate model capabilities on performing intra-weight injection. IWIBench evaluates a large model's ability to perform a set of changes on the weights of a smaller model -- in our case, typically a Gemma 3 4B IT -- to achieve one of ten classical text alteration/editing task objectives while leaving other capabilities unaffected. Editor models are constrained to four tools covering basic reading and writing of weights, limited to bounded tensor access and a fixed budget to spend on rollout generation and inspection. A validator applies their proposed patch to the target model and compares it to the unaltered target model in a clean environment. 

Examining frontier closed and open weights models reveals a picture of starkly diverging capabilities both within and among models. A limited number of frontier models perform very significantly ahead of other contenders, most notably Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol. Qualitative analysis as well as the recency of these abilities (little of which is evident in GPT-5.6 Sol, for instance) suggests that this is a recent taught ability. Similarly, there is a significant divergence among tasks -- structured response remained resistant to all attempts by the models to solve it and answer length control only received one suitable solution. Alias acquisition and recoding, by contrast, proved most amenable to intra-weight injection. Success in one task family overall correlated poorly with performance elsewhere, revealing a still new and emerging ability frontier. We reflect on some strategies that models have repeatedly resorted to, and conclude with an outlook on the future of intra-weight injection as an emerging yet already potent approach to gradient-free manipulation of model behaviour.
\end{abstract}
